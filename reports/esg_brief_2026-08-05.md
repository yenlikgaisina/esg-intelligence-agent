## 1. Regulation Watch
*   **EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) Full Impact:** The EU CBAM is now fully impacting imports, with Kazakhstan's aluminum, iron, and steel sectors most affected. The International Trade Centre (ITC) estimates that if Kazakhstan's steel and iron exporters rely on EU default emissions values, the CBAM cost could surpass €108 million, representing up to 123% of some steel export values. However, monitoring, verifying, and reporting actual emissions could reduce this liability to approximately €57 million annually. (Source: The Astana Times, June 4, 2026)
*   **Kazakhstan's Domestic Carbon Pricing:** A critical factor exacerbating CBAM costs is Kazakhstan's significantly lower domestic carbon price of around €0.40 per ton, compared to the EU's €80 per ton. This disparity means Kazakhstan exporters effectively pay the full EU carbon price when entering the European market. Strengthening the domestic emissions trading system and aligning with EU standards could help reduce costs and retain carbon-related revenues domestically. (Source: The Astana Times, June 4, 2026)
*   **EU Green Public Procurement (GPP):** By 2026, both public and commercial projects within the EU are expected to comply with GPP standards, demanding transparent reporting on material origins, transport, and emissions. This directly impacts Kazakhstan exporters aiming for EU markets, requiring them to provide verifiable environmental data for their construction materials. (Source: Heiton Steel, August 5, 2026)

## Embodied Carbon Trends
The trend towards lower embodied carbon in materials is accelerating globally, driven by regulatory pressure (like EU GPP), investor demand, and corporate net-zero commitments. Key movements include:
*   **EPD Proliferation:** More manufacturers are obtaining and publishing EPDs, making it easier to compare and specify lower-carbon products.
*   **Steel Sector Innovation:** The steel industry is rapidly adopting Electric Arc Furnace (EAF) technology, utilizing high levels of recycled scrap (often over 90% in European mills) for significant carbon reductions. There are also early-stage developments in hydrogen-ready steelmaking, projected to scale by 2030.
*   **Buy Clean Policies:** Government procurement policies, such as "Buy Clean" initiatives in the US and EU GPP, are increasingly mandating embodied carbon disclosure and setting reduction targets for materials like concrete and steel.

## Recommended Actions
1.  **Prioritise CBAM Compliance & Data Accuracy:** Kazakhstan steel, iron, and aluminum exporters must immediately invest in robust systems for measuring, calculating, and independently verifying actual carbon emissions. This is crucial to avoid punitive default CBAM values and their substantial financial penalties (potentially saving over €50 million annually for steel exports).
2.  **Advocate for Domestic Carbon Price Alignment:** Engage with Kazakhstan government and industry bodies to advocate for strengthening the domestic emissions trading system. A higher domestic carbon price could align local regulations with EU standards, potentially reducing CBAM payments for exporters and retaining carbon revenues within Kazakhstan.
3.  **Demand & Provide EPDs:** For all construction materials, particularly steel and cement, clients should demand EPDs from their suppliers. Manufacturers and contractors supplying to or considering EU markets must develop and publish EPDs for their products to meet market expectations and regulatory requirements (e.g., EU GPP).
4.  **Explore Low-Carbon Material Sourcing:** Actively seek out and specify low-carbon material alternatives, such as steel produced via Electric Arc Furnaces with high recycled content. This is a direct pathway to reducing embodied carbon, mitigating CBAM risks, and aligning with global net-zero targets.
