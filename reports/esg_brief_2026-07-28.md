## 1. Regulation Watch
*   **EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) Full Implementation:** The transitional period for CBAM is ending in July 2026, moving to full implementation. This directly impacts imports of electricity, aluminium, iron and steel, and cement into the EU. Kazakhstan's exporters of steel and cement will now face a carbon levy based on embedded emissions, requiring detailed reporting. This aims to level the playing field for European producers and encourages global industrial decarbonization.
    *   **Who it affects:** Kazakhstan's steel and cement exporters, EU importers, and indirectly, all construction entities sourcing from or operating within the EU supply chain.
    *   **Effective Date:** July 2026 (full implementation).
    *   **Business Implication:** Kazakhstani exporters must establish robust systems for measuring and reporting embodied carbon in their products to avoid significant carbon levies and potential competitive disadvantage. Investment in decarbonization of production processes is critical.
    *   **Source:** *Web search summary (Assessment of impacts of the EU-CBAM)*, *ICAP Carbon Action (https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism-cbam-takes-effect-transitional-phase)*
*   **Tightening Procurement Rules in EU and UK:** Demand for greener materials is no longer aspirational but is now shaping government and major buyer procurement frameworks. These frameworks reward transparency, prioritize low-emissions performance, and make carbon data essential. The trend is moving from encouragement to expectation, with a clear direction that carbon intensity is becoming a key competitive edge.
    *   **Who it affects:** All construction material suppliers and contractors bidding on public and large-scale private projects in the EU and UK, and potentially other markets adopting similar standards.
    *   **Effective Date:** From 2026 onwards.
    *   **Business Implication:** Clients must ensure their supply chains can provide verified carbon data and demonstrate lower-emissions performance to secure future contracts.
    *   **Source:** *The Climate Group (https://www.theclimategroup.org/our-work/news/2026-year-rules-are-changing-steel-and-concrete)*

## Recommended Actions
1.  **Assess CBAM Exposure Immediately:** Kazakhstan-based clients exporting steel, cement, or related products to the EU must conduct an urgent assessment of their embodied carbon emissions and establish robust reporting mechanisms to comply with CBAM and mitigate financial liabilities from July 2026.
2.  **Prioritize EPD Development/Procurement:** Manufacturers should invest in developing EPDs for their key products, leveraging automation and AI where possible, to meet growing market demand and gain a competitive edge. Contractors and developers should prioritize specifying materials with readily available EPDs.
3.  **Explore Low-Carbon Material Innovations:** Actively research and pilot projects utilizing low-carbon concrete (especially those adhering to performance-based standards) and assess the viability of procuring green hydrogen steel as it becomes commercially available.
4.  **Integrate Embodied Carbon into Early Design:** Implement Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) tools and expertise at the earliest design stages to compare material options and lock in significant carbon reductions before construction begins.
5.  **Educate and Train Teams:** Provide targeted training for procurement, design, and project management teams on embodied carbon, CBAM implications, and the value of EPDs to foster a data-driven sustainability culture.
