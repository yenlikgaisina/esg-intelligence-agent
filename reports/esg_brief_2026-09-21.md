## 1. Regulation Watch

*   **EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) - Definitive Phase Commenced**
    *   **What it is:** The EU CBAM is now in its definitive phase. Financial liability for imports of covered goods (cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilizers, electricity, hydrogen) made from **January 1, 2026**, has begun. Importers must apply for "Authorized CBAM Declarant" status by **March 31, 2026**. CBAM certificates will go on sale from **February 2027**, and the first annual CBAM declaration and certificate surrender for 2026 imports are due by **September 30, 2027**. A binding de minimis exemption applies to total annual net imports below 50 tonnes of CBAM-relevant goods.
    *   **Who it affects:** Directly impacts Kazakhstan exporters of steel, cement, and aluminium to the EU, as well as EU importers of these materials.
    *   **Business implication:** Kazakhstan construction material exporters must immediately establish robust systems for tracking actual embedded emissions. Relying on conservative, penalizing default values, as specified in the December 17, 2025 implementing acts, will result in materially higher CBAM costs. Third-party verification of actual emissions is mandatory. Companies need to forecast CBAM costs as scenario-based estimates, updating projections with evolving EU ETS prices.
    *   **Source:** [CBAM 2026: Key Updates, Costs & What Companies Must Do](https://www.integritynext.com/resources/blog/article/mastering-cbam-compliance-in-2026-latest-updates-and-how-companies-should-prepare)

## Recommended Actions
1.  **Immediate CBAM Data Strategy for Kazakhstan Exporters:** For any steel, cement, or aluminium exports to the EU, immediately establish systems to collect and verify actual embedded emissions data. The cost of relying on punitive default values is substantial, and third-party verification will be mandatory.
2.  **Develop SBTi-Validated Decarbonization Plans:** Given CSRD findings, prioritize setting Science-Based Targets initiative (SBTi) validated GHG reduction targets, especially for Scope 3 emissions. Move beyond spend-based estimates to primary supplier data for Scope 3 to enhance disclosure credibility and attract investment.
3.  **Proactive Engagement with Low-Carbon Materials:** Actively explore and integrate low-carbon concrete, recycled steel, and other innovative materials into project designs and supply chains. With tightening procurement rules in key export markets (EU) and increasing investor demand, carbon intensity is a growing competitive edge.
4.  **Forecast CBAM Costs with Scenario Analysis:** Treat CBAM cost forecasts as dynamic, scenario-based estimates rather than fixed numbers. Regularly update projections based on evolving EU ETS prices and refined assumptions about product-specific benchmarks and carbon price recognition in the country of production.
